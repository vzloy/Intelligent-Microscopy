# Nikon Eclipse Ti2-E with AX R MP Confocal/Multiphoton System

## System Reference for Imaging & Microscopy Workflows

This document describes a fully motorised inverted microscope platform (Nikon Eclipse Ti2-E) coupled with an AX R multiphoton/confocal scan head, a dual-output tuneable IR laser, six-line visible laser unit, NSPARC super-resolution detector, FLIM capability, and environmental control. It is intended as a machine-readable reference for designing acquisition workflows, analysis pipelines, and experiment planning with LLM-based tools.

**Assume the user is a beginner unless told otherwise.** The primary users of this system are MSc students in biomedical subjects (biology, biochemistry, medicine) who have little or no prior experience with confocal microscopy, laser physics, or NIS-Elements software. They know their biology — their cells, their fluorophores, their biological question — but they do not know how to translate that into microscope settings. Your response must bridge that gap completely.

**At the start of the conversation, after your first substantive answer, ask the user two calibration questions:**
1. *"Is this level of detail helpful, or would you prefer shorter and more technical responses?"*
2. *"How familiar are you with confocal microscopy — is this your first time, or have you used similar systems before?"*

Ask these once, adjust your style for the rest of the conversation, and do not ask again. If the user says they are experienced, switch to concise instructions without background explanations. If they say they are a beginner (or don't answer), continue with the full pedagogical approach.

This means:
- **Never leave logical or procedural gaps.** Do not assume the user knows what "sequential acquisition" means, why it matters, or how to set it up — explain each step and the reason behind it.
- **Be pedagogical.** Explain *why* before *how*. If you recommend Balanced channel series mode, briefly explain what bleed-through is and why it would corrupt their data before telling them which button to click.
- **Be comprehensive.** A beginner following your instructions should be able to sit down at the microscope and complete the experiment without needing to ask someone else to fill in missing steps.
- **Use plain language.** Avoid jargon without explanation. If you must use a technical term (e.g. "Nyquist sampling", "numerical aperture", "pile-up limit"), define it in one sentence the first time.
- **Adapt after calibration.** Once the user answers the two opening questions, match their level for the rest of the conversation. An experienced postdoc gets concise click paths; a first-time MSc student gets the full explanation with context.

When a user asks how to perform an imaging experiment on this system, follow these priorities:

1. **Be user-oriented and GUI-oriented.** Give step-by-step NIS-Elements software instructions — which buttons to click, which menus to open, which settings to change, and in what order. Refer to §16 (Standard Software Workflows) first. Users need to know *what to do in the software*, not which hardware component handles the photons.

2. **Be application-oriented.** Start from the user's biological question (sample, fluorophores, what they want to measure), then map it to the correct software workflow. Do not lead with hardware specs unless the user specifically asks about hardware.

3. **Verify dichroic and laser routing internally.** Before recommending a confocal experiment, silently check §4.9 (First Dichroic Mirror Turret) to verify that the laser-dichroic combination is valid. **Do not tell users to check or select the dichroic themselves** — NIS-Elements automatically selects the correct dichroic position when users create experiments via the "Based on Dyes" wizard (§16.1). The dichroic information in §4.9 is for your internal validation and for advanced users / engineers only. If the user's fluorophore combination would require a dichroic change mid-experiment (e.g. both 488 nm and 445 nm lines), explain that NIS-Elements will handle this automatically via Multiple Passes — do not ask the user to manually switch dichroic positions.

4. **Include practical tips and warnings.** Check §15 (Operational Procedures & Constraints) for relevant rules: power-on sequence, NSPARC alignment requirement, stitching warm-up, FRAP galvano-only constraint, resonant scanner denoise-after-stitch rule, pile-up limits, etc.

5. **Use hardware details only when necessary.** The detailed hardware specifications (§1–§13) are for engineers, facility managers, and advanced users troubleshooting optical paths. For routine experiment planning, the software workflows (§16), operational procedures (§15), and capability summary (§17) are sufficient.

6. **HCF4 is not just a confocal microscope.** It is a versatile imaging platform. Do not default to recommending confocal for every experiment. Consider whether the user's application is better served by:
   - **Widefield fluorescence via camera** (ORCA-Flash4.0 v3 + D-LEDI) — gentler than laser scanning; ideal for very sensitive live-cell and live-tissue imaging where phototoxicity must be minimised; faster whole-field capture; excellent for screening, time-lapses of slowly changing processes, and samples that bleach quickly.
   - **Resonant confocal scanning** — fast (up to 30 fps at 2048×512), gentle (lower per-pixel dwell time), with Denoise.ai for real-time noise removal. Use as the **default scanner for any speed-dependent acquisition**: fast 3D Z-stacks, time-lapses, multipoint experiments, large-image tiling, and live-cell dynamics (membrane trafficking, calcium waves, cell migration). Resonant should be the first choice whenever acquisition speed, sample viability, or throughput matters.
   - **DIC / phase contrast via camera** — label-free live imaging of cell morphology, migration, division. No fluorescence bleaching at all. Combine with a fluorescence time-lapse using the T Phase channel reduction (§16.3.5) for occasional fluorescence snapshots.
   - **Galvano confocal scanning** — highest resolution (up to 8192²), best image quality, adjustable dwell time, and supports ROI scanning, polyline (1DT), scan rotation, and stimulation/FRAP. Use when **image quality is essential** (publication images, spectral imaging) or when galvano-only features are needed (ROI scan, polyline, rotation, stimulation). Galvano is slower but produces cleaner images without requiring Denoise.ai. Note: NSPARC super-resolution works with **both** resonant and galvano scanners — use resonant + NSPARC for faster super-resolution, galvano + NSPARC for maximum photon budget control.
   - **Multiphoton** — IR excitation confines fluorescence generation to the focal plane (intrinsic optical sectioning without a pinhole), reducing out-of-focus bleaching and phototoxicity. Use for: deep tissue imaging (less scattering than visible light), live-cell metabolic imaging with FLIM (NAD(P)H at 750 nm, FAD at 890 nm — avoids damaging UV excitation), organoid imaging, collagen SHG, and any experiment where the sample is sensitive to short-wavelength excitation. Multiphoton is not only for thick samples — it is often the gentlest way to image live cells with UV-excitable or endogenous fluorophores.
   
   When a user describes a live-cell experiment, ask what temporal resolution they need and how sensitive their sample is to light before recommending a modality. A 6-hour time-lapse of slowly dividing cells may be better served by widefield + camera than by confocal.

### 4.9 First Dichroic Mirror Turret

The AX R MP scan head (MHA52300) includes a motorised first dichroic turret with multiple positions. The quotation documents the following dichroics — the first four are included with the scan head, the fifth is a separately purchased pentaband add-on:

| Position | Dichroic | Code | Laser lines reflected | Primary use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Quad-band 405/488/561/640** | Included with MHA52300 | 405, 488, 561, 640 nm | **Standard confocal** — use for DAPI, GFP/AF488, TRITC/mCherry/AF568, Cy5/AF647 |
| 2 | **Pentaband 405/445/515/561/640** | MXU95086 | 405, 445, 514/515, 561, 640 nm | When using 445 nm and/or 514 nm laser lines (e.g. CFP, YFP) |
| 3 | **BS 20/80** | Included with MHA52300 | Any (reflects 20 %, transmits 80 %) | Low-reflection beamsplitter; useful for reflected-light or when maximum transmission to detector is needed |
| 4 | **IR composite dichroic** | Included with MHA52300 | IR (multiphoton wavelengths) | Two-photon / multiphoton imaging — reflects IR excitation, transmits visible emission |
| 5 | **Gold mirror** | Included with MHA52300 | IR (broadband) | Multiphoton mode — maximum IR reflection efficiency |

⚠️ **This section is for LLM internal validation and engineers/super-users only.** In normal operation, NIS-Elements automatically selects the correct first dichroic position when users create experiments via the "Based on Dyes" wizard. Users do not need to manually select or check the dichroic. The LLM should use the routing table below to silently verify that its recommendations are optically valid, but should not instruct users to check dichroic positions.

#### 4.9.1 Dichroic selection rules for LLM routing

| Laser line | Use quad-band (pos 1) | Use pentaband (pos 2) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 405 nm | ✅ | ✅ | Both dichroics reflect 405 nm |
| 445 nm | ❌ Not reflected — laser passes through | ✅ | **Only** the pentaband reflects 445 nm |
| 488 nm | ✅ | ❌ Not reflected — laser passes through | **Only** the quad-band reflects 488 nm |
| 514 nm | ❌ Not reflected — laser passes through | ✅ (as 515 nm notch) | **Only** the pentaband reflects ~514/515 nm |
| 561 nm | ✅ | ✅ | Both dichroics reflect 561 nm |
| 640 nm | ✅ | ✅ | Both dichroics reflect 640 nm |

**Key consequence:** Alexa Fluor 488, GFP, FITC, and any fluorophore excited at 488 nm **require the quad-band dichroic (position 1)**. The pentaband does not reflect 488 nm. Conversely, CFP (445 nm excitation) and YFP (514 nm excitation) **require the pentaband dichroic (position 2)**. The quad-band does not reflect 445 or 514 nm.

If an experiment requires both 488 nm and 445 nm excitation, the acquisition must be split into sequential runs with a dichroic change between them (or use the BS 20/80 at the cost of 80 % excitation loss).

For multiphoton imaging, use position 4 (IR composite) or position 5 (gold mirror). The NDD sits before the scan head and is not affected by the first dichroic — NDD-collected emission bypasses the turret entirely.

#### 4.9.4 General fluorophore selection rules

- Avoid fluorophores whose emission peak falls within ±10 nm of any reflected laser line on the active dichroic.
- For the tunable DUX-VB4, set detection windows within the transmission bands of the active dichroic.
- When using the NDD for multiphoton imaging, the first dichroic turret is bypassed — NDD notch restrictions do not apply.

## 15. Safety

### 15.1 Laser Safety

HCF4 is a **Class 3B laser system** (visible lasers) and uses a **Class 4 IR pulsed laser** (Insight X3+). Laser light is emitted from the objective, the diascopic detector port, and the external output port (when installed). **Invisible IR laser radiation is particularly dangerous** because you cannot see it.

**Mandatory rules:**
- **Never look into the objective or any open port** while any laser is active. Even reflected or scattered laser light can cause permanent eye damage.
- **Wear laser-protection goggles** when within the Nominal Ocular Hazard Distance (NOHD): 10.7 m for visible lasers, **19.2 m for the IR pulsed laser** on the AX-ISM-MP system.
- **Never place reflective objects** (mirrors, metal tools, foil) on the stage or in the illumination path — reflected laser light is as dangerous as direct exposure.
- The **laser interlock lid** (1OKH201D) must be in place during multiphoton imaging — it automatically shuts off the laser when lifted. Do not bypass or disable it.

### 15.2 IR Pulsed Laser (Multiphoton) — Spectra Physics Insight X3+ Dual

The Insight X3+ Dual is a **Class IV** high-power IR femtosecond pulsed laser. It produces two synchronized outputs at 80 MHz repetition rate:
- **Main output**: tuneable 680–1300 nm, <120 fs pulse width, >1.5–3.0 W (wavelength-dependent).
- **Dual output**: fixed 1045 nm, <170 fs pulse width, >3.5 W.
- Both outputs can operate **simultaneously** (synchronized beams) for dual-wavelength multiphoton excitation.

Follow these rules without exception:

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **Shut the IR shutter when placing or removing specimens.** | IR laser light at 680–1300 nm is **invisible** — you cannot tell if it is on by looking. The 1045 nm dual output is also invisible. |
| **Shut the IR shutter whenever you are not actively acquiring images.** | Continuous IR exposure bleaches and damages samples, and poses a safety risk if someone reaches under the objective. |
| **Allow at least 30 minutes warm-up** after turning on the IR laser before imaging. Power reaches maximum within 30 minutes. For the **full stability specification** (<0.5 % drift in any 2-hour period), allow **60 minutes**. | The laser needs thermal stabilisation. The "Optimize" button in the GUI becomes available after 30 minutes. |
| **Always keep the IR laser main power and chiller ON** (even when not imaging). | The Insight X3+ is designed to stay powered continuously. The chiller (set to 21 °C) and power supply should remain on. Frequent power cycling shortens laser lifetime. Turn off emission and close the shutter, but leave main power on. |
| **Keep the room air-conditioned** (stable temperature, <75 % RH non-condensing up to 25 °C). | Temperature and humidity changes cause optical axis drift, power fluctuations, and can damage internal optics. Internal humidity must stay below 5 % (purge unit in power supply; replace every 2 years). |
| **VIS confocal and IR multiphoton scanning cannot run simultaneously through the scan head.** | The AX scan head has one VIS and one IR input sharing the optical path. Switch between lightpaths (AX vs AX MP) as needed. Note: this is a scan head limitation, not a laser limitation — the Insight X3+ Dual CAN output both tuneable and 1045 nm beams simultaneously. |
| **The two IR outputs (tuneable + 1045 nm) CAN be used simultaneously** for dual-wavelength multiphoton excitation. | The beams are synchronized at 80 MHz. Use for simultaneous excitation of two fluorophores at different IR wavelengths, or for combined SHG + two-photon fluorescence. Controlled via the Main and Sub shutters on the AX Pad (§17.3.1). |
| **Turn ON the IR laser emission** by clicking and holding the [On] button for **3–5 seconds** in NIS-Elements (AX MP GUI). Wait for the emission indicator to turn on. There is a 3-second safety delay before the diode laser energises. | Prevents accidental activation. |
| **Turn OFF the IR laser emission** by clicking and holding the [Off] button for **3–5 seconds**. Leave main power and chiller ON. | Shutter closes automatically when Off is issued. |
| **Set wavelength via NIS-Elements only.** After changing wavelength, wait for the "Actual Wavelength" indicator to match the requested wavelength before imaging. | The laser needs time to tune; imaging during tuning produces incorrect excitation. |
| **Re-align after every wavelength change** (Auto Alignment on AX Pad). | Beam position shifts slightly with wavelength. Without re-alignment, the image may be displaced or the NSPARC alignment lost. |

### 15.3 Pre-Operation Safety Checklist

Before starting any laser-based acquisition, verify:

- [ ] The AX scan head and all optical fibre cables are properly connected (not loose or disconnected).
- [ ] An **objective or a cap** is attached to every nosepiece socket — empty sockets leak laser light.
- [ ] A **cap or camera** is attached to the right-side port of the Ti2-E.
- [ ] The **protection plate** (supplied with the nosepiece) is in place between the FL turret and the nosepiece.
- [ ] The **laser interlock lid** is closed on the cage incubator (if using multiphoton).
- [ ] No reflective objects (metal tools, foil, mirrors) are on or near the stage.
- [ ] If switching from DIC to multiphoton: **remove the DIC analyser** from the optical path (it blocks/reflects IR light and can be damaged).

### 15.4 Mechanical Safety — Preventing Objective and Sample Damage

On the inverted Ti2-E, objectives are **below** the sample. The motorised Z-drive, XY stage, and nosepiece rotation can all cause crashes that damage objectives (£2,000–£15,000 each) or destroy samples.

**Preventing Z-axis crashes (objective hitting sample holder):**
- When placing or removing a sample, always **lower the objective** first (or use the **Swap** position — the green line in Z Navigation — which moves to a safe Z height automatically).
- After changing objectives (especially switching from a short WD lens to a long WD lens), **lower Z before rotating the nosepiece**. A longer objective may collide with the stage insert.
- Use **PFS** for automatic focus — it locks onto the coverslip and prevents the objective from driving into it. If PFS loses lock (flashing indicator), stop Z movement immediately.
- When setting up Z-stacks, set the **Bottom position first** (closest to objective), then Top. Starting from Bottom reduces the risk of over-driving into the sample.
- For the **piezo Z stage** (500 µm range): the software limits travel, but be aware that the main Z-drive has no built-in collision detection.

**Preventing XY crashes (objective hitting stage frame):**
- When using high-magnification objectives with short working distances (60× Oil at WD 0.15 mm, 40×C Sil at WD 0.30 mm), **lower Z before making large XY stage movements**.
- The stage travel range (114 × 73 mm) extends beyond the sample holder opening — at the edges, the objective can contact the metal frame of the stage insert.
- When running automated multipoint or tiling experiments, **verify all XY positions are within the sample area** before starting acquisition (especially with JOBS or large-image workflows that visit many positions automatically).

**Preventing nosepiece rotation crashes:**
- **Lower Z before rotating the nosepiece** to a different objective. Different objectives have different lengths — a longer objective will protrude further and may hit the sample holder or coverslip.
- If the nosepiece rotates to an empty socket (no cap), laser light escapes — always keep caps on unused sockets.

**Preventing immersion objective damage:**
- **Oil immersion** (60× Oil): apply a small drop of Immersion Oil F (MXA22168) to the objective front lens. Too much oil can run into the nosepiece mechanism. Clean after every session with lens paper and ethanol.
- **Water immersion** (20×C WI, 40×C WI): use the water immersion dispenser (MEV54006) for long sessions. Check the water meniscus periodically — if it breaks, the image goes black and you may need to refocus carefully.
- **Silicone oil** (25×C Sil, 40×C Sil): apply Silicone Oil (1ME37837, 500 cSt). Silicone oil does not evaporate and lasts longer than water, but must be cleaned off with lens paper before switching objectives.
- **Never use the wrong immersion medium** on an objective — oil on a water objective (or vice versa) degrades image quality and may damage optical coatings.

**If something goes wrong:**
- If you hear grinding or clicking from the Z-drive or nosepiece: **immediately stop all movement** (press Escape or turn off the Ti2-E).
- If an objective has contacted the stage frame or sample holder: **do not force it**. Lower Z slowly, then inspect the objective front lens for damage under the eyepieces.
- Report any suspected damage to FILM staff immediately. Do not continue imaging with a potentially damaged objective.

## 16. Operational Procedures & Constraints

This section contains practical operating rules and parameters that affect experiment design. It is derived from Nikon training documents for HCF4.

### 16.1 Power-On and Power-Off Sequences

**Power ON (confocal/multiphoton):**
1. Turn on PC.
2. Turn on laser bed (power button on rear, then turn key).
3. Turn on AX controller: press the main power switch (rear), then press the remote power switch (PUSH, front). The blue LED and STATUS indicator light **orange**, then blink **green** (initialising). A short beep sounds after ~10 seconds. Wait until the STATUS indicator becomes **solid green** and a 2-second beep confirms successful initialisation. If the STATUS indicator is **solid red**: an error has occurred — do not proceed; contact FILM staff.
4. Turn on Ti2-E microscope main body.
5. When Windows has loaded, start NIS-Elements.

**Power OFF:**
1. Close NIS-Elements.
2. Shut down PC.
3. Turn off Ti2-E microscope main body; wait for front LEDs to stop flashing, then turn off Ti2 controller.
4. Turn off AX controller (front button).
5. Turn the key on laser bed, then turn off power button on rear.

**Power ON (widefield only — no confocal):**
1. Turn on cameras (rear power button; wait for initialising LED to stop flashing).
2. Turn on D-LEDI (control pad power button).
3. Turn on Ti2 controller, then Ti2 main body (do not leave a long gap between them).
4. Turn on PC last.
5. Start NIS-Elements.

### 16.2 AX Pad — Key Acquisition Controls

| Control | Function | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Scanner | Resonant / Galvano | Resonant: fixed pixel dwell time; Galvano: adjustable dwell time (1.9–83 µs range) |
| Averaging | 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 | Can be switched to Integration in Device Setup |
| Channel Series | Fastest / Balanced / Minimal Crosstalk | See §15.5 |

### 16.3 NSPARC Imaging Protocol

⚠️ **Alignment must be performed before every important NSPARC acquisition.**

**Alignment procedure:**
- Use a sample with defined structure and good signal filling the FOV (avoid too much black background).
- Sample must be precisely in focus.
- Recommended settings: zoom 6, 561 nm laser.
- NSPARC alignment does NOT work with the 405 nm laser / blue channel.
- Auto-alignment works in both CF and SR mode; CF mode sometimes has a higher success rate.
- If signal is too low: increase laser power without excessive bleaching; open virtual pinhole; zoom in/out.
- When the alignment indicator turns green, acquisition is safe.

**Oversampling XY:**
- The blue square in the scan area window indicates the diffraction-limited pixel size. Zoom in until inside the blue square to break the diffraction limit.
- Recommended pixel size: **≤ 50 nm** with 60× or 100× high NA objectives.
- Pixel size should be **2.3× smaller** than the expected resolution after reassignment and deconvolution.
- Pixel density adjustable: 512×512, 1024×1024, 2048×2048.

**Photon budget (brightness):**
- Intensity in the super-resolved region must stay **below the pile-up limit** of the NSPARC detector. Exceeding this makes reassignment imperfect and data non-quantifiable.

| Scanner | Pile-up limit formula / guideline | Recommended photons |
|---|---|---|
| Galvano | 2091 × pixel dwell time (µs) × number of integrations | 300–400 photons; minimum 50 for reassignment |
| Resonant 1K×1K | ~15–20 photons without integration | Use integration to reach ≥ 50 |
| Resonant 512×512 | ~30–40 photons without integration | Use integration to reach ≥ 50 |
| Resonant 256×256 | ~60–80 photons without integration | Use integration to reach ≥ 50 |

- Galvano mode: saturation indicator shows pile-up pixels in false colour.
- Resonant mode: saturation indicator does NOT show pile-up.
- Virtual pinhole can be opened to collect more photons (reduces XY and Z resolution).

**Oversampling Z:**
- Z-step size: **120–130 nm** for 60× and 100× objectives (2.3× smaller than expected axial resolution).
- Required for proper deconvolution.

**Reassignment:** Use SR NSPARC mode.

**Deconvolution settings for NSPARC data:**
- Apply 3D deconvolution.
- Modality: **NSPARC SR Mode**.
- Pinhole size: **1 AU** (if virtual pinhole was 1 AU during acquisition).
- Noise level: **Clear** or **Low Noise** (test different levels if artefacts appear).
- Iterations: **< 10** with Automatic Stopping.
- Recommended algorithms: **Richardson-Lucy** (first choice), Automatic, or Blind.
- Weak out-of-focus signal may cause deconvolution artefacts — limit Z-stack range or use single-band emission filters to reduce crosstalk.

|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-Channel | Simple fluorescence experiments | Up to 4 dyes + TD | Dye-based wizard auto-selects lasers and detectors |

### 16.5 Channel Series & Multiple Pass Modes

**Channel Series** (linewise switching — within a single pass):

| Mode | Behaviour | Speed impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Fastest** | All channels scanned simultaneously; each line scanned 1×. | No speed penalty |
| **Balanced** | Channels grouped in pairs with sufficient spectral gap. | Frame rate reduced by number of line-groups |
| **Minimal Crosstalk** | Each channel scanned separately (linewise switching from longest to shortest emission). | Frame rate reduced by number of channels |

**Multiple Passes** (framewise switching — when >4 channels or detector reuse needed):

| Mode | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Simultaneous** | All passes acquired at once (if hardware permits) |
| **Fast Sequential** | Channels grouped into fewest passes possible; GaAsP detectors reused with different tunable emission ranges between passes |
| **Quality Sequential** | Each channel gets its own pass, using the same detector for consistent calibration; maximum crosstalk rejection |

Channel Series indicated by Arabic numbers in the spectral graph; Passes indicated by Roman numerals.

### 16.6 Stimulation and FRAP

⚠️ **On HCF4, FRAP and photostimulation are only possible as Sequential Stimulation in Galvano Scanning Mode.** The AX R does not have a hybrid scanner. Simultaneous stimulation (using the resonant scanner) would require an OMS scanner attached to LAPP ports with dedicated stimulation lasers — this is not installed on HCF4.

**Stimulation settings:**
- Configured in the AX Stimulation dockable window.
- Per-configuration parameters: laser line, laser power (%), pixel dwell time (µs).
- Unlimited stimulation configurations can be stored (no 3-config restriction on AX).
- ROIs are drawn in Live/Frozen window and assigned to stimulation configurations via right-click → "Assign to AX".
- PMT gain behaviour during stimulation: **"Keep Gain"** recommended for sequential stimulation.

**FRAP workflow:**
1. Acquisition phase (pre-bleach time series).
2. Bleaching/Stimulation phase(s) — one or more, each assigned a stimulation configuration.
3. Acquisition phase (post-bleach recovery time series).

**FRAP analysis:**
- Time Measurement with Background ROI (label "B") and Reference ROI (label "R") for photobleaching correction.
- FRAP button calculates half-maximal recovery time automatically.

### 16.7 Large-Image Stitching — Practical Rules

**Two stitching approaches:**
1. **Multipoint → post-acquisition stitch** (ND Acquisition or JOBS → stitch later via ND Processing, GA3, or JOBS right-click). Preserves raw unstitched tiles; allows testing different stitching methods.
2. **Large Image on-the-fly** (ND Acquisition or JOBS Large Image commands). Simpler but raw tiles may not be preserved.

**Stitching methods:**

| Method | Behaviour | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Optimal Path** | Finds least-difference contour in overlap region | Most samples (try first) |
| **Blending** | Averages overlapping pixel intensities | Homogeneous signal with little background |

**Image Registration (Precise Stitching):** Refines XY tile alignment beyond stage coordinates. Adds processing time but improves results. In JOBS, select the channel with the best contrast for registration.

**Overlap:** Default 10–15 %. Smaller overlap = less precise registration but fewer bleaching artefacts in overlap zone. 5 % acceptable if structures are well defined.

**3D stitching constraints:**
- Registration is done on ONE plane and applied to the full Z-stack.
- GA3/JOBS stitching uses EDF of the Z-stack for the registration map.
- ND Acquisition Large Image creates the registration map from the first Z-plane — ensure this plane has sufficient signal.
- For large 3D Z-stacks: acquire Z first, then XY (faster; stitching done post-acquisition).
- For 3D time-lapse: acquire XY first, then Z (reduces movement artefacts; only for slow dynamics).

**System preparation for stitching:**
- Warm up the AX controller for **≥ 3 hours** before stitching experiments.
- Clean the objective lens and coverslip (100 % ethanol or petroleum ether).
- Level the sample holder using PFS (tolerance ±3 µm).
- Re-calibrate objective after any exchange or cleaning.
- Move PFS dichroic out of the optical path (reduces shading).
- Remove DIC prism if not needed.
- Close condenser shutter to block LED autofluorescence; dim room lights.

**Resonant scanner stitching:** Acquire raw (un-denoised) images; apply Denoise.ai **after** stitching, not before.

**Immersion objectives:** May cause image distortion depending on immersion medium viscosity. Slow down XY stage speed. Water-dipping objectives may cause "wobbling" at final stage position — add a 1 s macro wait command before each tile acquisition.

**If stitching artefacts persist:** Zoom in slightly (1.4×) to use only the centre of the scan field.

**Shading correction:**
- Automatic (default in JOBS/GA3): computes correction image per channel. Fluorescence uses MinIP + filtering; brightfield uses MaxIP + filtering; DIC/PhC uses average + filtering.
- AX Shading Correction: pre-computed, hardware-specific, dedicated to certain objective lenses. Configurable in Device Setup → Scan Area.
- Manual: capture ≥ 10 reference images without sample at different positions.

   | **Maximum Spectral Range For Lasers** | Quick setup that assigns lasers based on the selected first dichroic mirror, maximising the spectral range per channel. |
   | **1 Excitation 2 Emissions (2×)** | Specialised: two excitation wavelengths in two passes producing 4 image channels. Use for ratiometric or dual-excitation experiments. |
   | **2 Excitations 1 Emission** | Specialised: two excitation wavelengths in two passes producing 2 channels sharing one emission window. |

6. For **Based on Dyes**: type the first dye name (e.g. "DAPI") in the search field → select the matching entry → press Enter. A new search field appears. Add remaining dyes the same way.
   - **Tip — Favorite Dyes**: click the ★ star icon next to any dye in the list to mark it as a favourite. Favourite dyes appear at the top of the list in future sessions, saving search time for dyes you use regularly.
7. Optionally tick **Use Transmitted Channel** to add a brightfield/DIC channel via the diascopic detector.
8. Click **Finish**.

**What the software does automatically:**
- Assigns the correct laser lines (405, 488, 640 nm for this example).
- Automatically selects the correct first dichroic position for the chosen laser combination — you do not need to check or change this yourself.
- Sets DUX-VB4 tunable detector emission bandwidths for each channel to avoid spectral overlap.
- Assigns detector types (GaAsP for green/orange channels, Multi-Alkali for blue and far-red).
- No Optical Configurations need to be created manually — the AX handles this internally.
- If your dye combination requires more than one dichroic setting (e.g. dyes needing both 488 nm and 445 nm excitation), the software will automatically split the acquisition into Multiple Passes — no manual intervention needed.

**After the preset is created:**
- The Experiment Setup window shows a **Spectral Graph** at the bottom with emission curves, laser positions, and detector windows.
- Set **Channel Series** mode (see §16.2).
- Click **Save As...** to name and store the preset. It appears in the AX Pad dropdown for quick recall.
- Click **Close** to return to the AX Pad.

**Built-in automatic presets** (available without any setup):

| Preset | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Search Sample** | Uses all available lasers and the fastest scanning mode to find any fluorescent signal on the sample. Use this when you first put your sample on the stage and need to locate your cells. Settings can be adjusted; click **Reset** to restore defaults. |
| **Full Spectra** | Uses all lasers and minimal resolution to acquire a quick spectral overview image as fast as possible. Useful for checking which wavelengths your sample emits before designing a full experiment. Requires DUX-VB. |
| **Last Used** | A temporary preset auto-created when settings change unexpectedly (e.g. loading an old optical configuration). ⚠️ This preset can be overwritten by the software at any time — if you want to keep it, right-click and select **Duplicate** or **Rename** immediately. |

**Managing presets:**
- Right-click any preset name to **Duplicate**, **Delete**, **Rename**, or set it as **Private** (visible only to you) or **Shared** (visible to all users).
- Drag preset names to reorder them in the AX Pad dropdown menu.

### 16.2 Choosing the Channel Series Mode

The Channel Series dropdown in the Experiment Setup (and optionally on the AX Pad via "Show on Pad") controls how multiple channels are acquired within each scan line.

| Mode | What it does | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Fastest** | All lasers fire simultaneously; each line scanned once. Maximum frame rate. | Speed-critical live imaging where crosstalk is acceptable. |
| **Balanced** | Channels grouped in spectrally distant pairs (e.g. DAPI+AF647 together, AF488 separately). Lines scanned 2× per frame. | **Default recommendation.** Good compromise: reduced crosstalk without excessive speed loss. |
| **Minimal Crosstalk** | Each channel scanned separately per line, longest emission first. Lines scanned N× per frame (N = number of channels). | Quantitative colocalisation, FRET donor/acceptor, any experiment where channel bleed-through must be eliminated. |
| **Custom** | User manually assigns channels to arbitrary line groups and software passes. Full control over acquisition order. | Advanced users who need non-standard channel groupings (e.g. grouping by sample sensitivity rather than spectral distance). |

**Practical example (DAPI + AF488 + AF647, Balanced mode):**
- Pass 1, line group 1: 488 nm → AF488 (GaAsP, 500–550 nm)
- Pass 1, line group 2: 405 nm + 640 nm → DAPI (MA, 425–475 nm) + AF647 (GaAsP, 663–738 nm)
- Frame rate = ½ of Fastest mode.

**Automatic Experiment Settings buttons** (available in Edit mode for DUX-VB presets):

These calculate the optimal assignment of lasers, channels, detectors, and passes across all possible combinations — more thorough than Channel Series alone:

| Button | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Simultaneous** | Fastest possible; similar to Fastest channel series |
| **Fast Sequential** | Reasonable quality and time; similar to Balanced |
| **Quality Sequential** | Best quality; uses the same detector for each channel across passes for calibration consistency; similar to Minimal Crosstalk |

**When to use Multiple Passes** (framewise switching):
- When the experiment uses >4 channels, at least one detector must be reused with different emission settings between passes.
- Enable via the **Allow Multiple Passes** checkbox in Experiment Setup.
- Passes shown as Roman numerals (I, II, III) in the Spectral Graph; Channel Series as Arabic numbers (1, 2, 3) within each pass.

  - On **DUX-ST** (filter wheel) detectors: a filter-changer control appears for selecting the installed filter cube.

4. Click **Save** or **Save As...** when finished.

**Edit Detector Emission on Pad:**

**Edit Laser Wavelength on Pad** (multiphoton only):
- Tick **Edit Laser Wavelength on Pad** in the edit mode.

**Show Channel Series on Pad:**
- Tick **Show on Pad** to add channel series mode buttons (Fastest / Balanced / Minimal Crosstalk) directly to the AX Pad for quick switching during live scanning.

### 16.2.2 Channel Management on the AX Pad

**Ch. Setup button:**
- Click **Ch. Setup** in the Acquisition panel to preview the channel acquisition order in live mode.
- When enabled, clicking a channel button activates the entire group that will be scanned together (e.g. in Balanced mode, clicking the DAPI channel also activates AF647 because they share the same line group).
- Useful for verifying that your channel series mode produces the expected groupings before starting a capture.

**Temporarily disabling a channel:**
- Right-click any channel button on the AX Pad and select **Off**.
- The channel is skipped during acquisition — no laser fires, no data captured for that channel — but the channel definition is preserved in the preset.

### 16.3 Adjusting Acquisition Parameters on the AX Pad

After creating the experiment preset, fine-tune parameters on the AX Pad:

| Parameter | Where | How to set |
|---|---|---|
| Scanner mode | AX Pad → Scanner | Click **Resonant** or **Galvano**. Resonant = fast, fixed dwell time; Galvano = adjustable dwell time (1.9–83 µs via Speed/Quality slider). The icon on the button shows scan direction (mono/bi-directional). |
| Pixel resolution | AX Pad → Scan Area | Select 2048, 1024, 512, or 256 (square); or 2048×1024, 1024×512, 512×256 (rectangular). Custom sizes available via "..." button. |
| Averaging | AX Pad → Averaging | Select 1, 2, 4, 8, or 16. Higher = better SNR but slower. Can be switched to Integration in Device Setup → Acquisition (frames are summed instead of averaged). |
| Pinhole | AX Pad → Pinhole | Set to **1.0 AU** for standard confocal sectioning; **0.7 AU** for enhanced resolution (dimmer image). Click the **AU** button to reset to the default value. The wavelength used for the AU calculation is displayed and can be changed in Configure. A second custom pinhole position button can be enabled in Configure. |
| Zoom | Scan Area pad → Zoom Size | Adjust to control pixel size and field of view. The **Nyquist** button auto-calculates optimal pixel size for the current objective and wavelength. |
| Laser power | AX Pad → per-channel slider | Drag the yellow slider. Click the laser icon to toggle the laser on/off per channel. Keep laser power as low as possible to minimise bleaching. |
| Detector gain | AX Pad → Gain slider | Set independently per channel. Increasing gain brightens the image but also amplifies noise. |
| Offset | AX Pad → Offset slider | Subtracts a constant intensity value from all pixels in the channel — reduces background noise. Show/hide via Device Setup → Acquisition tab. |
| Dwell time | AX Pad (Galvano only) | Drag the Speed ↔ Quality slider. Longer dwell = better quality but slower. Not available in Resonant mode (fixed dwell time). |

**Frame rate and frame duration** are displayed at the bottom of the scanning settings section. Change any scanning parameter and watch these values update in real time — useful for estimating whether your acquisition fits within a time-lapse interval.

**Key AI-powered controls:**

**AutoSignal.ai:**
- Click to automatically optimise laser power and gain for your sample.
- **Before running:** right-click the AutoSignal.ai button to **select your sample type** (e.g. fixed cells, live cells, tissue). The AI adjusts its strategy based on this selection.
- Laser power limits for each sample type can be configured in the Configure window (Device Setup → AutoSignal.ai).
- Uses TensorRT on the GPU.

**Signal / Shot Noise indicator:**
- Coloured rectangles displayed per channel showing the signal-to-noise ratio: **green** = good SNR, **amber** = moderate, **red** = low SNR.
- ⚠️ If the indicator is **red**, Denoise.ai may produce unpredictable results — increase laser power or averaging before relying on Denoise.ai.

**Denoise.ai:**
- Toggle on to apply real-time AI denoising to the live resonant image.
- The quality indicator below should be amber or green for effective denoising.
- Denoise.ai always respects the current Averaging setting.

**Smart Laser Off:**
- When enabled, the system monitors XY stage position, Z focus, and image brightness during live scanning.
- If no changes are detected for a set time, laser emission **stops automatically** and the live image is displayed as a **still frame** (the laser activity icon indicates the current state).
- Moving the stage or adjusting Z focus **restarts** live imaging for the active channel; other channels are cleared.
- Sequential wavelength (SW) passes are NOT auto-completed in this mode (hardware changes like filter/dichroic movements are too slow).
- Use this to protect samples from unnecessary bleaching during setup and navigation.

### 16.3.1 IR Laser Control (Multiphoton)

The IR laser section appears on the AX Pad when a multiphoton lightpath is selected:

**Turning the IR laser ON/OFF:**
- Click and **hold** the Emission switch for **3 seconds** to toggle ON or OFF.
- The colour indicator blinks while the laser is stabilising — wait until it becomes solid before imaging.

**Shutters:**
- **Main**: controls the variable-wavelength IR laser (Insight X3+ tuneable 680–1300 nm).
- **Sub**: controls the fixed-wavelength IR laser (Insight X3+ dual output at 1045 nm). Only visible in dual-IR configurations.

**Auto Alignment:**
- Tick **Automatic** to perform automatic XY shift alignment every time the IR laser wavelength is changed (important — image will be displaced without alignment).
- Click **Run** to trigger alignment manually at any time.
- ⚠️ Always re-align after changing the IR wavelength.

**Humidity monitor:**
- Some IR lasers report internal humidity.
- If the value is displayed in **red**: there may be a problem with the IR laser body. **Stop using the laser immediately** and contact Nikon service or the supplier.

**Synchronised power for multi-channel multiphoton:**
- When a single IR laser is used for multiple emission channels (e.g. NAD(P)H and FAD both excited at the same IR wavelength), the laser power sliders and ON/OFF switches are synchronised across all channels sharing that laser.

### 16.3.2 NSPARC Control

The NSPARC section appears when a lightpath using the NSPARC detector is selected:

**Detector modes:**

| Mode | Behaviour | Alignment needed? |
|---|---|---|
| **SR Mode** | Super-resolution: the resulting pixel value is calculated from the 5×5 detector array using a reassignment algorithm. Best image quality and resolution. | Yes — alignment must be green |
| **CF Mode** | Confocal: the resulting pixel value is the sum of all 5×5 array elements. Functions as a high-sensitivity confocal detector. | No |
| **MP Mode** | Multiphoton: same summation as CF Mode but optimised for IR-excited emission. | No |

**Live Deconvolution for SR Mode:**
- Tick this option to perform **real-time deconvolution** on the live image while in SR Mode. Shows the final super-resolved result immediately during scanning — useful for focusing and framing before capture.

**Include Array Components:**
- Tick to save the **raw 5×5 detector array data** alongside the processed image.
- This data can be reprocessed later using General Analysis 3 (e.g. with different reassignment parameters).
- Only available if "Allow Storage of NSPARC Array Components" is enabled in the Configure window.

**Virtual Pinhole:**
- The NSPARC detector does not use the physical pinhole. Instead, an **adjustable zoom unit** has a similar effect — controlling the trade-off between resolution and brightness.
- Slide toward smaller values for better resolution (dimmer); toward larger values for more brightness (lower resolution).
- In **MP Mode**, a **Max** button appears — press it to transmit the maximum available light to the detector.

**Saturation monitoring (Resonant mode):**
- **Saturation Value**: the recommended maximum pixel intensity calculated from current acquisition parameters. Keep your signal below this value to avoid pile-up.
- **Oversaturated Ratio**: percentage of pixels exceeding the saturation value in the last captured image. If this is high, reduce laser power.

**Starting acquisition:**
- **Live**: continuous scanning (for finding and adjusting).
- **Find**: fast preview using line-skipping (faster than Live, for navigating the sample).
- **Capture**: single-frame capture with current settings.
- **ND Acquire**: opens the ND Acquisition dialog for multi-dimensional experiments (Z-stack, time-lapse, multipoint, large image, or combinations).

### 16.3.4 ND Acquisition — Common Options

ND (N-Dimensional) Acquisition is the multi-dimensional experiment engine in NIS-Elements. Open it via **ND Acquire** in the Acquisition panel, or via **Applications → 6D → Define/Run ND Acquisition**. Each dimension adds a tab:

| Dimension | Symbol | Tab | What it captures |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time-lapse | **T** | Time | Sequence of images over time |
| Multipoint | **XY** | Points | Images at different stage positions |
| Z-series | **Z** | Z | Stack of images at different focal planes |
| Multi-channel | **λ** | Lambda | Different fluorescence channels (confocal) or optical configurations (camera) |
| Large image | **M** | Large Image | Tiled scan stitched into a single image |

Tick one or more tabs to create a combined experiment (e.g. a "TXY Z" experiment = time-lapse + multipoint + Z-stack). The **Order of Experiment** dropdown controls the nesting order (see §16.6).

**Save to File:**
- Tick **Save to File** to auto-save during acquisition (recommended for long experiments).
- Set the **Path** (folder) and **Filename**.
- By default, images are saved as **ND2** files. You can switch to **single or multipage TIFF** by changing the filename extension — useful for compatibility with third-party analysis tools.

**Custom Metadata:**
- Tick **Custom Metadata** to attach structured metadata (treatment names, conditions, concentrations, notes) to every image captured.
- Click **Edit** to create or modify a metadata set:
  - **New**: create a named metadata set.
  - **Add New**: add metadata items (text fields, numbers, dropdowns).
  - **Export/Import**: save metadata sets as XML files for sharing between users or experiments.
- Metadata can be edited after acquisition in **File → Image Properties**.
- Use this for experiment documentation — it is stored inside the ND2 file and is searchable in the Organizer.

**Autofocus in ND Acquisition:**
- Select the autofocus method from the dropdown in the ND Acquisition window. Each method has independent settings configurable via the **Define** button:

| Method | Behaviour | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Fast Automatic** | Auto-selects the fastest strategy (piezo vs Z drive, continuous vs step-by-step, single vs two-pass) | Default for most experiments |
| **Steps in Range** | Discrete Z steps within a defined range; evaluates focus criterion at each step | When continuous mode fails (low contrast, noisy signal) |
| **Continuous in Range** | Continuous Z sweep; evaluates focus on the fly | Fast focusing for well-defined samples |

**Close Active Shutter options** (reduce photobleaching between captures):

| Option | When shutter closes | Use case |
|---|---|---|
| **Close Active Shutter when idle** | Between time points, between positions — any pause in acquisition | General bleaching reduction for live samples |
| **Close Active Shutter during Filter Change** | While filters or dichroics switch between channels | Multi-channel experiments where filter switching takes time |
| **Close Active Shutter during Stage Movement** | While the XY stage moves between multipoint positions | Multipoint experiments, especially with slow stage movement |

Tick one or more of these in the **Advanced** section of the ND Acquisition window.

**Execute macro commands during ND acquisition:**

Custom macros or NIS-Elements commands can be inserted at six points in the acquisition cycle:

| Insertion point | When it runs |
|---|---|
| **Execute before each Time** | Before each time-lapse frame |
| **Execute after each Time** | After each time-lapse frame |
| **Execute before Time phase** | Once before the entire time phase starts |
| **Execute after Time phase** | Once after the entire time phase ends |

**Z Intensity Correction:**
- Tick **Enable Z Intensity Correction** to compensate for signal loss with depth in Z-stacks.
- Uses the global correction profile defined in **View → Acquisition Controls → Z Intensity Correction**.
- The correction increases laser power or gain progressively at deeper Z positions.
- Requires the JOBS module.

**Load/Save experiment configuration:**
- Click **Save** to export the entire ND Acquisition dialog configuration as an **XML file**.
- Click **Load** to restore a saved configuration.
- Use this to share experiment templates between users or to quickly recall a complex setup for repeat experiments.

### 16.3.6 Combined ND Experiments — Running and Monitoring

When multiple ND dimensions are combined (e.g. Time + Z + XY + Lambda), these features help you verify, monitor, and control the experiment.

**Before starting — Experiment Timing:**
- Click the **Experiment Timing** button (clock icon) in the ND Acquisition window.
- A window shows: **expected total duration**, **total number of frames**, and **number of time-lapse frames**.
- ⚠️ Always check this before clicking Run now — verify the experiment fits within your booking slot and that the file size is manageable.
- The ND Acquisition window header also shows a **live preview of the ND2 file structure** as you tick/untick dimension tabs.

**Order of Experiment:**
- Click the **Order of Experiment** button above the dimension tabs to set the nesting order.
- Example: **Z series(T (Time))** = full Z-stack at each time point; **T(Z series)** = time-lapse at each Z-plane (rarely useful).
- The order affects acquisition speed and motion artefacts — see §16.7 for guidance.

**During acquisition — ND Experiment Progress window:**

The progress window appears automatically when the experiment starts. It shows: overall progress, time elapsed, estimated remaining time, and **remaining free disk space**.

| Button | Action |
|---|---|
| **Next Loop** | Skip the remaining wait period and start the next time loop immediately. Only enabled when the system is waiting between time points (interval longer than acquisition time). |
| **Next Phase** | After the current loop finishes, jump to the start of the next time phase (skipping remaining loops in the current phase). |
| **Start Phase** | Select a specific phase from the dropdown and jump to it. |
| **Pause / Continue** | Pause the experiment (all hardware stops). Click again to resume from where it paused. |
| **Refocus** | Pauses the experiment and starts **Live** mode so you can manually refocus. Close Live to resume the experiment. Use when PFS has lost lock or the sample has shifted. |
| **Finish** | Stops the experiment and **saves** all data captured so far. Use when you have enough data or need to end early. |
| **Abort** | Stops the experiment **without saving**. All unsaved data is lost. Use only as a last resort. |
| **Events** | Insert timestamped event markers into the running experiment. Same as §16.7.2. |

**Modifying ROIs during acquisition:**
- Right-click any ROI in the image during a running ND acquisition to access a context menu.
- ROIs (including stimulation/FRAP ROIs) can be **duplicated**, **deleted**, or **moved** while the experiment continues.
- Use case: if a cell migrates out of the original FRAP ROI during a time-lapse, move or re-draw the ROI to follow it without stopping the experiment.

**Recorded Data:**
- Click **Record Data** in the ND Acquisition window before starting.
- Select which metadata to record: timestamps, device positions (Z, XY, PFS offset, filter positions, laser power), and custom items.
- Choose recording frequency: **Per Frame** (every single frame), at a **Frequency** (every Nth frame), or at a **Time** interval.
- After acquisition, view recorded data via **File → Image Properties → Recorded Data** tab.
- Useful for quality control, correlating hardware state with image features, and documenting exact acquisition conditions.

**Data safety on interruption:**
- If the experiment finishes normally, all data is saved to the ND2 file.
- If you click **Cancel** during the "Please wait. Finishing ND experiment, writing image data to hard disk" message, only data already physically written to disk is preserved. The remainder is lost.
- ⚠️ Always use **Finish** (not Abort) if you want to keep partial data from an interrupted experiment.

### 16.3.5 Multi-Channel in ND Acquisition (Lambda Tab)

When multi-channel acquisition is combined with other ND dimensions (time-lapse, Z-stack, multipoint), the **Lambda** tab in ND Acquisition provides channel-specific optimisations that can significantly reduce acquisition time and bleaching.

**Channel setup:**
- Each row in the Lambda tab represents one channel with an assigned **Optical Configuration** (OC). Select an existing OC from the dropdown, or create a new one via **<define new...>**.
- **Comp. Color**: sets the display colour for the channel.
- **Focus Offset**: per-channel Z offset for parfocality correction (same as §16.11.4).
- For AX confocal experiments, multi-channel is normally handled by the Experiment Presets (§16.1) rather than the Lambda tab. The Lambda tab is primarily used for **camera-based multi-channel** imaging or when mixing camera and confocal channels.

**T Phase column — reduce channels across time points:**

When **T (Time)** and **Lambda** are both active, a **T Phase** column appears. Each channel can be set to:

| Option | Behaviour | Example use |
|---|---|---|
| **All** | Captured at every time point | GFP — need full temporal resolution |
| **First** | Captured only at the first time point | DAPI — only needed once as a reference |
| **every n-th** | Captured every Nth time point | Brightfield — check cell morphology every 10th frame |

⚠️ At least one channel must be set to **All** — the system needs at least one channel at every time point.

This is one of the most effective ways to reduce bleaching and file size in long time-lapses. For example, in a 6-hour time-lapse: capture GFP every 5 minutes (All), but DAPI only at the first and last time points (First + a macro to capture at the end).

**Z Pos column — reduce channels across Z-planes:**

When **Z** and **Lambda** are both active, a **Z Pos** column appears. Each channel can be set to:

| Option | Behaviour | Example use |
|---|---|---|
| **All** | Captured at every Z-plane | GFP — need full 3D information |
| **Home** | Captured only at the Home Z position | Brightfield — only need one focal plane |

⚠️ At least one channel must be set to **All** in the Z Pos column.

This saves substantial time when only one channel needs a full Z-stack but others only need a single plane. For example: acquire a GFP Z-stack for 3D reconstruction, but capture the DIC/brightfield reference only at the focal plane.

**T Phase and Z Pos can be combined** freely — e.g. a channel set to "First" (T) and "Home" (Z) is captured once, at the first time point, at the focal plane only.

**Manual Capture:**
- **Acquire → Capture Multichannel Image → Capture Manually** opens a live window with a **Capture** button.
- Press Capture to acquire one channel. The system switches to the next channel automatically.
- After all channels are captured, a **Recapture** button appears. Click a single channel to re-do just that one.
- Click **Finish** when satisfied.
- Use when optical configuration switching needs manual intervention (e.g. physical filter swap) or when you want visual confirmation of each channel before proceeding.

**Wait for user before changing to next channel:**
- In the Advanced section, tick this option to pause between channels and wait for user confirmation.
- Use for experiments requiring manual sample manipulation between channels (e.g. adding a reagent, removing a coverslip, adjusting a perfusion valve).

**Per-channel autofocus and macros:**
- In the Advanced section, the **"Advanced for"** dropdown allows setting different autofocus methods and macro commands for each channel independently.
- Click **Apply to All** to use the same settings across all channels.
- ⚠️ If the Lambda tab has only one channel combined with other dimensions (T, XY, Z), the per-channel Advanced settings are skipped for optimisation.

**Merge Cameras** (when using both HCF4 cameras in one experiment):
- By default, two separate ND2 files are created (one per camera).
- Tick **Merge Cameras** to combine into one ND2 file (using the maximum width, height, and bit depth from both cameras).
- Tick **Stretch Camera images to Same Size** if the cameras have different resolutions.

### 16.4 Setting Up a Z-Stack

1. In the Acquisition panel, click **ND Acquire**.
2. Tick the **Z** checkbox in the ND Acquisition window.
3. Choose one of the **three Z-definition modes** (click the corresponding button — the active one turns blue):

**Mode 1 — Defined by Top and Bottom:**
- Run **Live**. Move the Z drive to the top of your structure → click **Top**. Move to the bottom → click **Bottom**.
- Enter the **Step** size in µm, or specify the number of **Steps**. Click **Use suggested step size** to auto-calculate the Nyquist-optimal step for the current objective and wavelength.
- The **Home** position (where the Z drive returns after acquisition) is automatically set to the middle step.
- **Keep exact position**: the last Z position (top or bottom) may shift slightly due to step arithmetic. Right-click the **Top** or **Bottom** button → select **"Keep exact Top Position"** or **"Keep exact Bottom Position"** to guarantee which end is preserved exactly.
- Click **Reset** to discard Top, Home, and Bottom settings and start fresh.

**Mode 2 — Symmetric (defined by Range):**
- Move the Z drive to the centre of your structure → click **Home**.
- Enter the total **Range** (µm) — the Z-stack will extend equally above and below Home.
- Enter **Step** size or number of **Steps**. Click **Use suggested step size** for Nyquist-optimal.
- **Relative mode**: if you don't know the exact centre in advance, switch to **Relative** — the Home position will be taken from the current Z position at the moment acquisition starts (not from the stored absolute position). Useful when combined with PFS or autofocus.

**Mode 3 — Asymmetric (defined by Range):**
- Move to centre → click **Home**.
- Enter separate **Below** and **Above** ranges (µm) — the Z-stack extends asymmetrically around Home.
- Enter **Step** size or number of **Steps**.
- Use this when the structure of interest is not centred in Z (e.g. cells sitting on the coverslip — more range above than below).

4. **Z Device**: select from the dropdown:
   - **Ti2 Z-drive** (main Z) — 20 nm step resolution, full travel range.
   - **Piezo** (Mad City Labs Nano-Z500-N2) — faster, sub-nm resolution, 500 µm range.
5. Tick **Close Active Shutter during Z Movement** to reduce bleaching.
6. Set direction: **Bottom to Top** (default) or Top to Bottom.
7. Click **Run Z Corr** to measure and correct stage backlash before acquisition.
8. Click **Run now** to start.

**Two Z-device auto-combination** (directly relevant — HCF4 has both devices):
- If **Piezo Z** is selected in the Z device dropdown and the defined **Range fits within 500 µm**: only the piezo is used (fastest Z movement).
- If **Piezo Z** is selected but the **Range exceeds 500 µm**: the software **automatically combines both devices** — the piezo handles fast fine Z stepping within its range, while the main Z drive repositions for the coarse range. This significantly speeds up large Z-stacks.
- If **Main Z** is explicitly selected: only the main Z drive is used regardless of range.

**Piezo button actions** (in the Z-series dialog when a piezo is connected):
- **"Keeps Z position and centers Piezo Z"**: moves the piezo to its home position while compensating with the main Z drive to maintain the same absolute Z position.
- **"Move Piezo Z to Home position"**: resets the piezo to home regardless of the main Z position.

**Recommended Z-steps by objective and application:**

| Objective | Confocal 1 AU (µm) | NSPARC (µm) |
|---|---|---|
| 20× WI NA 0.95 | ~1.0 | ~0.5 |
| 25×C Sil NA 1.05 | ~0.8 | ~0.4 |
| 40×C Sil NA 1.25 | ~0.5 | ~0.25 |
| 40×C WI NA 1.15 | ~0.55 | ~0.25 |
| 60× Oil NA 1.42 | ~0.35 | 0.12–0.13 |

### 16.5 Setting Up Multi-Point Acquisition

Multi-point acquisition captures images at multiple XY stage positions in a single experiment. Open via **ND Acquire** → tick **XY (Points)**. Requires a motorised XY stage (installed on HCF4).

#### Defining points

**Point by Point (Manual):**
1. Move the stage to the first position of interest.
2. Click **Add New** — a row with the current XY(Z) coordinates appears in the point list.
3. Move to the next position and repeat until all positions are defined.
4. **Point names**: click the name field to rename from default (#1, #2, #3) to meaningful labels (e.g. "Control well A1", "Treated well B3").

**Well Plate (Rectangular) pattern:**
1. Click **Custom...** → select the **Well Plate** tab.
2. Choose:
   - **Manual**: enter Rows × Columns and Distance X/Y (mm) between wells. Scanning starts at the current position.
   - **Interactive**: specify number of fields, then follow the wizard to click the top-left and bottom-right corners — distances are auto-calculated.
3. Click **Finish**.

**Large Image (Covering) pattern:**
1. Click **Custom...** → select the **Large Image** tab.
2. Define the **Scan Area** by number of fields or by actual area size (mm).
3. Select the **Objective** — the field count adjusts based on magnification and FOV.
4. Set **Overlap** (%) between neighbouring fields.
5. Click **Finish**. The result is a grid of multipoint positions that can later be stitched into a large image.

**Random Multi-Point** (for unbiased quantitative sampling):
1. Click **Custom...** → select the **Random** tab.
2. Choose the area shape:
   - **Rectangle**: define X, Y of top-left corner, width and height.
   - **Radius**: define X, Y of centre and radius.
3. Set the **Number of points** to generate.
4. Click **Finish**. Random positions are distributed within the defined area.

#### Point list management

| Feature | How | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Include Z** | Tick the checkbox | Stores a Z coordinate per point — enables per-position focus offsets for uneven samples |
| **Relative XY** | Tick the checkbox | All coordinates stored relative to the current stage position instead of absolute. Right-click any point → **"Set this point as a reference position"**. Use for reproducible experiments on different samples. |
| **Optimize** | Click the **Optimize** button | Reorders all points to **minimise XY stage trajectory**. Significantly reduces travel time in large multipoint experiments. |
| **Offset All** | Select a point → move stage to new position → click **Offset All** | Shifts ALL point coordinates by the same XY(Z) offset. Use when a sample has been repositioned or removed and replaced. |
| **Move Stage to Selected Point** | Toggle button (keep pressed) | Clicking a point in the list moves the stage to that position. Essential for verifying and adjusting individual points. |
| **Update Z for one point** | Select the point → adjust Z drive → click **←** button | Updates only the Z coordinate of the selected point to the current Z position. XY coordinates cannot be adjusted (delete and re-add instead). |
| **Save / Load** | Click **Save** or **Load** | Export the point list as an **XML file**; reload it for repeat experiments or share between users. |

#### Advanced multipoint options

| Option | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Leave PFS offset ON between points** | Keeps PFS engaged while the stage moves between points. Faster than disengage → move → re-engage at each position. Recommended for most live-cell multipoint experiments. |
| **Redefine reference Z after Auto Focus** | Updates the reference Z position after autofocus or PFS runs at each point. Keeps subsequent Z-stacks properly centred even if the sample has drifted. |
| **Use Focus Surface** | Uses a pre-defined focus surface (§16.15) instead of per-point Z coordinates or autofocus. Best for flat but tilted samples. |
| **Split Multipoints** | Automatically splits the resulting ND2 file into individual files (one per point) after acquisition. Useful when each position needs to be analysed independently or shared with different collaborators. |
| **Define Stimulation Laser Powers** | When checked, a dialog appears during ROI definition allowing you to set laser power per stimulation group at each multipoint position. Use for experiments where different positions receive different stimulation intensities. |

#### Execute commands during multipoint acquisition

In the **Advanced** section:
- **Execute Command before Capture**: runs a macro or command before each position is captured (e.g. trigger a perfusion valve, log metadata).
- **Execute Command after Capture**: runs after each position (e.g. export data, update a tracking sheet).

See §16.3.4 for the full list of macro insertion points.

### 16.6 Setting Up a Tiled Large Image

**Method 1 — ND Acquisition (on-the-fly stitching):**
1. Click **Large Image** in the Acquisition panel.
2. The Large Image Scan dialog opens.
3. Set fields: number of tiles in X and Y, or define by area size in mm.
4. Select the objective from the dropdown.
5. Set **Overlap**: 10–15 % (default). Reduce to 5 % for bleaching-sensitive samples.
6. Set **Stitching via**: **Optimal Path** (default, best for most samples) or Blending.
7. Tick **Precise Stitching (Image Registration)** for sub-pixel alignment.
8. Optionally tick **Automatic Shading Correction** → select **Black Background (Fluorescence)**.
9. Optionally tick **Use PFS** for focus maintenance across the tile grid.
10. Click **Scan** to start.

**Method 2 — ND Acquisition + post-acquisition stitch (preserves raw tiles):**
1. Click **ND Acquire** → tick **XY (Points)**.
2. Click **Custom...** → switch to **Large Image** tab.
3. Define the tile grid (fields, overlap, objective).
4. Click **Finish** → **Run now**. Individual tiles are saved.
5. Post-acquisition: **Image → ND Processing → Stitch Multipoint to Large Image**.
6. Adjust stitching parameters (Optimal Path / Blending, Image Registration channel, shading correction).
7. This approach allows trying different stitching methods on the same raw data.

**Method 3 — JOBS (recommended for repeat experiments):**
1. In JOBS Editor, add a **Generate Points** task → set Point Placement = **Covering**, Overlap = 15 %, Scan Direction = **Meander**.
2. Add a **Z-Stack Loop** if 3D stitching is needed (acquire Z first, then XY for large 3D datasets).
3. Add a **Capture** task.
4. Add a **Stitch** task → set Stitching via = Optimal Path, tick Precise Stitching, tick Automatic Shading Correction → Black Background.
5. Run the job.

**Additional JOBS large image tasks:** Scan Large Image in Slide/Well/Area (scans within a defined holder region), Scan Large Image in Region (scans within drawn ROIs).

**Additional entry points for large image acquisition:**

| Command | Location | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Scan Large Image** | Acquire menu | Most comprehensive large image dialog |
| **Manual Large Image** | Acquire menu | Manual area selection by clicking/dragging |
| **Large Image tab** | ND Acquisition | Combined with other ND dimensions (T, Z, XY, λ) |
| **JOBS tasks** | JOBS Editor | Scan Large Image, Scan in Slide/Well/Area, Scan in Region |
| **Scanning Wizard** | Addons → Large Images | Guided step-by-step wizard for simple scans |
| **Scanning Wizard EDF** | Addons → Large Images | Wizard with Extended Depth of Focus |
| **ND Acq + Large Images** | Addons → ND Acquisition | Combined ND + Large Image addon |

#### 16.6.1 Scan Area Definition Options

In the ND Acquisition Large Image dialog, the scan area can be defined three ways:

| Method | How | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Frame array** | Set number of rows × columns | When you know the tile grid size |
| **Area size** | Enter width × height in mm | When you know the physical sample area |
| **Pattern from XML** | Click Browse → select an XML file | Non-rectangular scan areas (e.g. tissue outlines). Create the XML using the `_LargeImageGrabbingFreeShape` macro. |

#### 16.6.2 PFS Per-Tile Offsets

When **Use PFS** is ticked, a **PFS column** appears in the tile list with per-tile PFS offset values:
- Click the **←** button next to a tile to store the current PFS offset for that position.
- Right-click the PFS column header for:
  - **Offset All Points in PFS**: shift all tile offsets by the same amount.
  - **Reset All PFS Offsets**: set all tiles to the current PFS offset value.
- Use per-tile offsets when the coverslip is slightly tilted or uneven — each tile gets its own focus lock point.

#### 16.6.3 Wait Before Each Capture

In the Advanced section, set a **Wait before each capture** delay (in ms or seconds):
- The system pauses after each XY stage movement or autofocus before capturing the tile.
- Use for **immersion objectives** where the liquid meniscus needs time to settle after stage movement (see also the wobbling workaround in §15.7).
- Also useful after autofocus if the Z-drive has mechanical settling time.

#### 16.6.4 Distortion Correction.ai

AI-based geometric distortion correction for camera-based large images. Corrects duplicated structures at tile overlap boundaries caused by optical distortion in relay lenses (most common with non-1× zoom relay lenses).

**Calibration (one-time per hardware configuration):**
1. Place a focused, bright, dense sample on the stage — it must cover the entire camera field of view.
2. ⚠️ Do NOT use: out-of-focus samples, samples not covering the FOV, or samples with periodic/repeating structures (e.g. calibration grids).
3. Right-click the Live image → **"Create Distortion Correction Maps for Current Settings"**.
4. The computed distortion maps are saved and tied to the current configuration (microscope port, objective, camera ROI, wavelength, zoom).
5. Repeat for each hardware configuration you use for large images.

**Application:** In the Scan Large Image dialog, enable the Distortion Correction.ai option. The maps are applied automatically during stitching if the current settings match a calibration. Requires the Local Option module.

#### 16.6.5 Camera ROI Interaction

⚠️ When a **Camera ROI** is active on the Live image, the large image scanner uses **only the ROI frame area** (not the full sensor) for each tile. This means:
- Tile size is reduced to the ROI dimensions.
- More tiles are needed to cover the same area.
- Turn off Camera ROI before large image scanning if you want full-sensor tiles.

**⚠️ Resonant scanner stitching rule:** Acquire **raw (un-denoised) images**. Apply Denoise.ai **after** stitching, not before.

### 16.7 Setting Up a Time-Lapse

1. Click **ND Acquire** → tick **T (Time)**.
2. Set **Interval** (time between frames) and **Duration** or **Loops** (total number of frames).
3. For live-cell imaging:
   - Engage **PFS**: in the Ti2 Pad, click **PFS → IN**. Set the PFS offset to lock focus on the coverslip.
   - Turn on the **cage incubator** (37 °C) and set the **CO₂ controller** to the required concentration (typically 5 % for standard mammalian cell culture). Wait for the in-chamber sensor reading to stabilise at the target value before starting acquisition.
   - If using a water immersion objective, fill the **water immersion dispenser** and verify the nozzle is positioned over the objective.
   - Enable **Smart Laser Off** on the AX Pad to minimise photobleaching between frames.
4. For multi-position time-lapse:
   - Tick **XY (Points)** → add positions manually or load from a well-plate definition.
   - The system will visit each position at every time point.
   - Check that the total acquisition time per cycle (all positions × all Z slices × all channels) fits within the interval.
5. Click **Run now**.

**Combining dimensions:** In the ND Acquisition window, the **Order of Experiment** dropdown controls nesting. For time-lapse + Z: select **Z series(T (Time))** to acquire a full Z-stack at each time point. For time-lapse + tiling: select **Large Images(T (Time))** or use JOBS for more control.

#### 16.7.1 Multiple Time Phases

The time-lapse table allows defining **consecutive phases** with different intervals and durations. This is essential for drug treatment experiments:

Example:
- Phase 1: every 1 min for 10 min (pre-treatment baseline)
- Phase 2: every 30 sec for 5 min (during drug addition — faster sampling to capture the response)
- Phase 3: every 5 min for 2 hours (recovery — slower sampling to reduce bleaching)

**How it works:**
- Each row in the table is a phase. Click **Add** to add more phases.
- For each phase, **Duration**, **Interval**, and **Loops** are linked — set any two and the third is calculated automatically.
- Select/deselect phases in the first column checkbox to include or exclude them.
- A **priority flag** (column header icon) indicates which parameter is preserved if the camera exposure time exceeds the requested interval. Click the Duration or Loops column header to set the priority.

#### 16.7.2 Events — Marking Experimental Interventions

During a running time-lapse, you can insert **timestamped event markers** into the ND2 file to document experimental interventions (drug addition, medium change, stimulation, temperature shift, etc.).

**Setting up events:**
1. In the ND Acquisition window, click **Events...** in the Advanced section.
2. Define events: click in the **Hot key** field and press the key combination to assign (e.g. F5).
3. Type a description (e.g. "Drug added").
4. Optionally assign a macro command that runs automatically when the event is triggered.
5. Mark one event as the **default** (radio button in the rightmost column) — this is inserted whenever the **Insert** key is pressed during acquisition.
6. Tick **ND Panel** for any event to make it visible in the ND Control Panel (View → Acquisition Controls → ND Control Panel).

During acquisition, press the assigned hot key at any time. A marker is placed on the ND2 timeline and the optional macro runs.

⚠️ Event hot keys take priority over all other keyboard shortcuts while a time-lapse, time measurement, or Time ND document is active.

#### 16.7.3 Keep Object in View

For samples where the object of interest moves (migrating cells, swimming organisms), **Keep Object in View** automatically tracks the object and moves the XY stage between frames to keep it centred.

1. In the ND Acquisition Advanced section, tick **Keep Object in View**.
2. Click **Settings...** → select the channel with the best contrast on the object.
3. Move the object to the centre of the field of view.
4. Adjust camera/illumination for high contrast, low noise (improves tracking accuracy).
5. Run the acquisition — the stage repositions automatically between frames.

Requires motorised XY stage (installed on HCF4) and the Advanced 2D Tracking module.

#### 16.7.4 Autofocus Timing in Time-Lapse

In the Advanced section, the autofocus timing option determines when focusing occurs:

| Option | Behaviour | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **At the Beginning** | Autofocus runs once before the experiment starts | Single-phase experiments; or when PFS handles ongoing focus |
| **Before each Phase** | Autofocus runs at the start of each time phase | Multi-phase experiments where focus may shift between phases |

⚠️ If your experiment has **only one time phase** and "Before each Phase" is selected, **autofocus will NOT run**. Use "At the Beginning" instead.

#### 16.7.5 Per-Phase Macro Commands

Different macro commands can be executed for each time phase independently:

1. In the Advanced section, select a phase from the **"Advanced for"** dropdown.
2. Set the macro commands for that phase (Execute before/after each Time).
3. Repeat for other phases, or click **Apply to All** to use the same commands everywhere.

Use **Execute Every Nth...** from the dropdown to run a macro every Nth time point instead of every one — reduces overhead in fast time-lapses (e.g. run autofocus every 10th frame instead of every frame).

#### 16.7.6 Transmitted Illuminator Management

Tick **Switch Transmitted Illuminator off When Idle** to turn the DIA lamp off during long gaps between acquisitions (reduces phototoxicity and lamp wear).

⚠️ Some lamps need warm-up time to reach stable intensity. If you see inconsistent brightfield illumination at the start of each time point, add a `Wait()` macro command before capture to allow the lamp to stabilise.

#### 16.7.7 Fast Time-Lapse — Understanding Timing Limits

When designing sub-second time-lapses, understand the three timing regimes:

| Regime | Interval setting | Camera mode | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **No delay** | Set to "No delay" | Internal hardware trigger (streaming) | Camera sends frames at maximum speed. If the PC can't keep up, frames may be **dropped**. Use for maximum fps when every frame doesn't need to be saved. |
| **Interval too short** | Interval < (exposure + readout + SW overhead) | Software trigger | The actual interval is **stretched** to the minimum achievable: exposure time + sensor readout time + software overhead. The requested interval cannot be met — the Duration or Loops priority flag determines which parameter is preserved. |
| **Interval sufficient** | Interval ≥ (exposure + readout + SW overhead) | Software trigger | Timing in the resulting ND2 file **matches** the requested interval exactly. |

For confocal time-lapses (AX scan head), the equivalent constraint is: the interval must be longer than the frame time × number of channels × number of Z-slices. The frame rate display on the AX Pad (§16.3) shows the current frame time — use it to verify your interval is achievable before starting.

### 16.8 Post-Acquisition Processing

**Denoise.ai (single frame or batch):**
1. Menu: **NIS.ai → Denoise.ai** (or for Z-stacks/time-lapse: **NIS.ai → ND Denoise.ai**).
2. Select which channels to denoise.
3. Tick **Create new document** to preserve the original.
4. Click **OK**.
5. For ND Denoise.ai: select **Correlated Noise** for resonant scanner data with line noise, **Uncorrelated Noise** for standard point noise, or **Automatically** to let the software decide.

**Deconvolution:**
1. Menu: **Deconvolution → 3D Deconvolution...** (or 2D for single planes).
2. Settings:
   - **Deconvolution Method**: Richardson-Lucy (recommended first), Automatic, Blind, Landweber, or MAP.
   - **Modality**: Confocal, or NSPARC SR Mode for NSPARC data.
   - **Pinhole Size**: match the value used during acquisition (1.0 AU default).
   - **Noise Level**: Clear (for pre-denoised data), Low Noise, or Medium Noise.
   - **Iterations**: ≤ 10 with **Auto Stopping** ticked.
3. Verify objective parameters (Magnification, NA, Immersion Ref. Index) are correct.
4. Click **Deconvolve**.
5. If Denoise.ai was already applied, set noise level to **Clear**.
6. Tip: keep the same deconvolution parameters for all images that will be compared quantitatively.

**Clarify.ai (widefield de-blurring):**
1. Menu: **NIS.ai → Clarify.ai**.
2. No parameters to set — it is parameterless.
3. For noisy images, combine with Denoise.ai (run Denoise.ai first, then Clarify.ai).
4. For well-sampled confocal data, use standard deconvolution instead.

### 16.9 NSPARC Super-Resolution Workflow

1. Select the **60× Oil NA 1.42** or **40×C Sil NA 1.25** objective (high NA required).
2. Set up the experiment preset as in §16.1 using your dyes.
3. **Align NSPARC** (required before every important acquisition):
   - In the AX Pad, click **NSPARC: Alignment → Auto Align**.
   - Use zoom 6, 561 nm laser (recommended). Do NOT use 405 nm / blue channel — alignment will fail.
   - Wait for the indicator to turn **green**.
   - If alignment fails: try CF mode instead of SR mode; increase laser power slightly; adjust zoom; open virtual pinhole; use a different sample, then switch back.
4. Switch to **SR Mode** (NSPARC super-resolution mode).
5. Set pixel size ≤ **50 nm** (use the Nyquist button or type directly in Scan Area → Pixel Size). The pixel size must be **2.3× smaller** than the expected resolution after reassignment + deconvolution.
6. Check **brightness**: intensity must stay below the pile-up limit (see §15.3 photon budget table). Recommended: 300–400 photons (galvano), ≥ 50 minimum for reassignment.
7. Set Z-step to **120–130 nm** for 60×/100× objectives if acquiring a Z-stack.
8. **Capture** the image.
9. Post-acquisition: apply **3D Deconvolution** with Modality = NSPARC SR Mode, Pinhole = 1 AU. Recommended method: **Automatic** (auto-selects the best algorithm, usually Richardson-Lucy). If using Richardson-Lucy directly, enable Auto Stopping with ≤ 10 iterations (see §16.8).

#### 16.9.1 How to Work with NSPARC — Step-by-Step Operating Instructions

(From Nikon instruction manual M855 E7, §4.3.4)

**Before you start — prepare the environment:**
1. Check that no air conditioning vent or cooling fan is blowing directly onto the NSPARC unit. Move fans away or redirect airflow if needed.
2. Remove any equipment with vibration sources (laptop fans, pumps, centrifuges) from the vibration isolation table and its frame.
3. Once you start scanning, do not touch the vibration isolation table or anything on it. Even light contact degrades NSPARC resolution.

**Set the correct pixel size:**
1. Look at the **Nyquist(NSPARC)** value displayed in the AX Scan Area panel.
2. Set your **Pixel Size** to a value between **half of Nyquist(NSPARC)** and **Nyquist(NSPARC)**. Example: if Nyquist(NSPARC) = 0.111 µm, set pixel size between 0.055 and 0.111 µm.
3. The **light blue square** in the AX Scan Area shows the FOV that corresponds to the Nyquist(NSPARC) pixel size — zoom in until your scan area fits inside or matches this square.

**Set the correct brightness (resonant scanner):**
1. Switch LUTs to **Rainbow Contrast**.
2. Set the LUT upper limit to the maximum brightness for your pixel count:

| Pixel count | Max brightness (1 integration) |
|---|---|
| 512 × 512 | 25 |
| 1024 × 1024 | 12 |
| 2048 × 2048 | 6 |

3. Start **Live** scanning. If the area of interest appears **pink** (exceeding the limit), **reduce laser power** until the signal stays below the limit.
4. The image will look dim — that is normal. Increase the **number of integrations** to build up the signal-to-noise ratio to an acceptable level.
5. These brightness limits are specific to NSPARC in CF Mode with the resonant scanner. They are separate from the galvano pile-up photon counts in §15.3.

**For multiphoton NSPARC (MP-NSPARC) — manage the Virtual Pinhole:**
1. If Virtual Pinhole is set to 1–2 AU and your imaging FOV is larger than the **MP-NSPARC view field** (the cyan rectangle in AX Scan Area), you may see increased shading at the edges.
2. Fix this by either: setting Virtual Pinhole to **"Max AU"** (transmits maximum light), or switching to the **NDD optical path** instead of NSPARC for that field of view.

**For NSPARC large images (tiling) — add a wait time:**
1. When tiling with NSPARC at high zoom, the specimen may appear to shake in captured tiles because the stage hasn't fully settled.
2. In the Large Image Advanced settings, tick **Wait before each capture** and set it to **500–1000 ms**.
3. If shaking persists, increase the wait time further.

**For multiphoton NSPARC — do not adjust internal optics:**
- During MP-NSPARC imaging, do not adjust these parameters — changing them will prevent Auto Alignment from working:
  - Collimator lens in the AX MP scan head
  - Shift mirror in the incident optical unit
  - Focusing unit inside the incident optical unit
- If you see a dark image after someone has adjusted these, restore the original values and contact FILM staff or Nikon service.

### 16.10 FRAP / Photostimulation Workflow

⚠️ On HCF4, FRAP is **galvano-only, sequential stimulation only**.

1. Switch scanner to **Galvano** in the AX Pad.
2. Set up your imaging channels (e.g. GFP at 488 nm).
3. Open **AX Stimulation** dockable window (Acquisition Controls or Menu → Applications → Define/Run Sequential Stimulation).
4. Create a **Stimulation Configuration**:
   - Click **+** to add a new configuration (e.g. name it "Bleach").
   - Select the laser line(s) for bleaching (e.g. 488 nm at 100 %).
   - Set the **Pixel Dwell Time** for the stimulation phase (higher = more effective bleaching).
5. In the Live/Frozen window, draw ROIs using the ROI toolbar.
6. Right-click the ROI → **Assign to AX → Bleach** (your stimulation configuration name).
7. In Device Setup → Acquisition, set **Gain Behavior during Stimulation** to **Keep Gain**.
8. Open **ND Stimulation** (Menu → Applications → Define/Run Sequential Stimulation):
   - Phase 1: **Acquisition** — set interval, duration, loops (pre-bleach imaging).
   - Phase 2: **Bleaching** — assign the Stimulation Configuration ("Bleach"). Set loops (number of bleach scans).
   - Phase 3: **Acquisition** — set interval, duration, loops (post-bleach recovery).
9. Optionally define a **Background ROI** (label "B") and a **Reference ROI** (label "R", right-click → Define as Reference region) for photobleaching correction.
10. Tick **Perform Time Measurement** for inline intensity tracking.
11. Click **Run now**.
12. Post-acquisition: in the Time Measurement window, click the **FRAP** button to auto-calculate half-maximal recovery time. Manually adjust max-bleached and max-recovery time points if needed.
13. For quantitative FRAP analysis, use **Define FRAP Calibration...** in the Time Measurement tools to set calibration parameters (mobile fraction, diffusion coefficient fitting).

#### 16.10.1 Photoactivation and Photoconversion

The same sequential stimulation workflow as FRAP is used for photoactivation and photoconversion experiments — only the biological context and laser settings differ:

| Application | Laser line | Proteins | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Photoactivation** | 405 nm | paGFP, PA-mCherry, PA-TagRFP | Protein becomes fluorescent in the stimulated ROI; track diffusion/transport from activated region |
| **Photoconversion** | 405 nm | Dendra2, mEos2/3, Kaede, mMaple | Protein shifts emission colour (green → red) in the stimulated ROI; track converted pool separately |
| **FRAP (photobleaching)** | 488 / 561 nm (high power) | GFP, mCherry, any fluorophore | Fluorescence destroyed in the ROI; measure recovery rate to determine mobility |
| **Uncaging** | 405 nm (or multiphoton IR) | Caged compounds (caged Ca²⁺, caged glutamate, caged ATP) | Bioactive molecule released locally; observe downstream response |

**Workflow** — identical to §16.10 steps 1–12, except:
- For photoactivation/conversion: set the 405 nm laser to the stimulation configuration at moderate power (typically 5–20 %; too high causes bleaching instead of activation).
- For multiphoton uncaging: use the IR laser in the stimulation configuration (requires AX MP lightpath).
- Post-stimulation imaging uses a different channel (e.g. activate with 405 nm, image the activated protein with 488 nm or 561 nm).

#### 16.10.2 Creating Stimulation ROIs from Segmentation

Instead of drawing ROIs manually, you can create them from a segmented binary layer:

1. Segment your image (e.g. threshold nuclei, run Segment Objects.ai, or use GA3).
2. The result is a binary layer with detected objects.
3. Convert the binary to ROIs: **ROI → Create ROI from Binary** (or right-click the binary → Convert to ROIs).
4. Right-click the ROIs → **Assign to AX → [your stimulation configuration]**.
5. This enables automated stimulation of segmented objects — e.g. bleach every detected nucleus, or photoactivate only cells in a specific region.

#### 16.10.3 Stimulation Modes Available on HCF4

| Mode | Available? | How | Use case |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sequential Stimulation** | ✅ Yes | AX galvano scanner; acquire → stimulate → acquire | FRAP, photoactivation, photoconversion, uncaging |

#### 16.11.6 Camera Shading Correction

If widefield camera images show uneven illumination (bright centre, dark edges), apply a shading correction:

**Simple Shading Correction** (one correction for all optical configurations):
1. Open **Acquire → Shading Correction Panel**.
2. Select **Simple** mode.
3. Click **Capture** — a dialog guides you through the process:
   - Find an empty, clean area of the glass slide.
   - Move slightly out of focus.
   - Select the background type: **Bright**, **Dark**, or **Neutral** (match your imaging modality).
   - Click **Next >>** to capture an image. Repeat ~10 times, moving the stage between captures. The images are merged into a single correction image.

1. Find an empty clean area, defocus the image.
2. **Acquire → Capture** a single image (the dust spots will be visible as dark blobs).
3. In the Shading Correction panel, click **Use Current Image as Shading Image**.
4. The correction will compensate for the dust pattern in all subsequent captures.

**Testing before applying:**
- In the Shading Correction panel, switch to the **Test On Current Image** tab and click **Test** to preview the correction effect on the current image before committing.
- An intensity profile graph shows the illumination uniformity before and after correction.

#### 16.11.7 Ring Buffer — Capturing Fast Unpredictable Events

The Ring Buffer (RAM Capture) continuously records the live camera image to RAM. When an unpredictable fast event occurs (calcium spark, vesicle fusion, membrane rupture, cell division), press Capture and the system saves frames from **before and after** the trigger moment — you don't need to react in time, the pre-event frames are already in the buffer.

**Setup:**
1. **Acquire → RAM Capture → Settings**.
2. Set **Frames buffered before** (e.g. 50 frames) and **Frames buffered after** (e.g. 50 frames). Alternatively, switch to **Time** and set seconds.
3. Click **OK**.

**Workflow:**
1. **Acquire → RAM Capture → Circular Buffer ON** — the toolbar indicator shows the buffer is active and continuously recording.
2. Start **Live** and watch the sample.
3. When the event of interest occurs, press the **RAM Capture** button (or **Acquire → RAM Capture → Capture**).
4. A new image sequence opens containing the buffered frames — both the pre-event and post-event window.

**Notes:**
- Buffer size is limited by available RAM. Larger buffers = more frames but more memory.
- If you press RAM Capture while in **Frozen** mode or with the buffer turned **OFF**, only the "after" frames are captured (no pre-event history).
- This is a **camera-only** feature — it uses the ORCA-Flash4.0 v3 or Basler camera, not the AX confocal scanner.
- For predictable events with known timing, use a standard time-lapse (§16.7) instead.

### 16.16 Focus Surface for Tilted or Uneven Samples

Use when stitching a thin sample that is not perfectly flat (tilted slide, uneven mounting).

**Using ND Acquisition / JOBS Add-on:**
1. Open **XYZ Overview** (View → Acquisition Controls → XYZ Overview).
2. Navigate to several positions across the tile area and record the Z position at each (the Focus Surface tab shows a map).
3. Define ≥ 3 reference points (JOBS requires >2 for interpolation; a rectangular area defined by only 2 points triggers a warning).
4. In the JOBS-based scanning add-on, activate **"Use Z for Focus Surface"** checkbox.
5. The system interpolates Z between reference points and adjusts focus per tile.

**Alternative:** If focus surface setup is too complex, slightly open the **Pinhole** (e.g. 1.5–2 AU) to increase the depth of field and tolerate mild sample tilt.

**For thicker samples** (>10 µm), use a small Z-stack (5 slices) per tile position instead of a focus surface, and create a MaxIP after stitching.

### 16.17 Stitching Troubleshooting — Tiled View Check

If a stitched image shows artefacts (visible seams, intensity steps, misalignment):

1. Re-acquire the same area as individual multipoints: **ND Acquire → XY (Points) → Custom → Large Image tab** → set grid and overlap → **Run now**.
2. After acquisition, switch to **Tiled ND View** (View menu or ND toolbar → tiled layout).
3. Inspect the individual unstitched tiles for inhomogeneities: uneven illumination, edge darkening, stripy patterns, or bleaching gradients.
4. If individual tiles look good but stitching is bad: try different stitching parameters (Optimal Path vs Blending, different registration channel, different overlap).
5. If individual tiles already show artefacts: check objective cleanliness, condenser lens offset alignment, shading correction, and PFS dichroic position.

**Condenser Lens Offset alignment check** (for stitching quality):
1. Place a red fluorescent Chroma slide on the stage.
2. Set the 561 nm laser, 20× Lambda D objective.
3. Decrease the pinhole size progressively.
4. If the hotspot is not centred: in **Device Setup → Pinhole**, adjust the **Offset Lens Position** (X and Y) in small steps until the hotspot is centred at 1 AU pinhole.
5. If you see regular horizontal stripes: adjust the Pinhole offset lens in **X direction only** in steps of 1 until the pattern is minimised.

### 16.18 FRAP — Practical Tips

**Bleaching efficiency and ROI placement:**
- The galvano scanner covers a rectangular bounding box over all ROIs assigned to the same stimulation configuration. Lasers are blanked outside ROIs via the AOTF.
- Assigning multiple **distant** ROIs to the same configuration reduces per-ROI bleaching efficiency (more pixels scanned, less dwell time per ROI pixel).
- Use **separate stimulation configurations** for distant ROIs; use the **same configuration** for nearby ROIs.
- There is no limit to the number of stimulation configurations on the AX.

**Testing bleach precision before the experiment:**
1. Set up your stimulation configuration and assign ROIs.
2. Click the **"Stimulate ROI"** checkbox at the bottom of the AX Stimulation window.
3. Set the ROI display to **100 % transparency** so you can see the bleach pattern on the live image.
4. Click **Stimulate** — the system bleaches only the ROI regions.
5. Verify that the bleach boundaries match your ROIs before starting the full FRAP experiment.

### 16.19 Automatic Correction Collar (Silicone/Water Objectives)

For the 25×C Sil, 40×C Sil, 20×C WI, and 40×C WI objectives (which have motorised correction collars on the Ti2-E):

1. Open **Device Setup → Correction Collar** (or use the dedicated AX + Ti2-E Automatic Correction Collar dialog).
2. The system scans through correction collar positions while measuring image sharpness.
3. The optimal collar position is set automatically for the current sample and cover glass thickness.
4. Re-run after changing the sample, cover glass, or imaging depth.

This compensates for refractive-index mismatch when imaging deep into tissue or through non-standard cover glass.
